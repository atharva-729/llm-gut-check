Should your LLM pipeline think first, and how much can skip human review?

Finding 1

The same three models give three different answers, depending on the task

For each task we found the cheapest auto-accept rule (API plus human review) that stays at least 95% correct (by a conservative lower bound). Rules were set on 200 items and tested unchanged on new ones. Total cost counts the API plus per human review of every item the rule doesn't accept.

Finding 2

Paying for thinking makes moderation worse, not better

A "flip" is an item where turning thinking on changed the answer. Green bars are fixes (wrong → right). Red bars are overthinking (right → wrong). On grammar, thinking mostly fixes. On moderation, it breaks about as much as it fixes, and Haiku breaks twice as much.

Can you trust "I'm 90% sure"?

Stated confidence vs reality, fast mode

On the dashed diagonal, a model that says 90% is right 90% of the time; points below it are overconfident. Marker size shows how many items fall in each band, and most items sit in the top band. In both tasks, answers the models give at 70–85% confidence are right less often than claimed. That's why a confidence cut-off has to be measured per task, never read off the model's own number.

The method

What a client gets

Probe. Check that each model's fast mode really does no hidden thinking, and that its answers parse.
Pre-register. Write the predictions and decision rules down and commit them before any test item is run.
Discover, then confirm. Set thresholds on one sample, then test them unchanged on fresh items.
Verdict card. Which mode, which model, which cut-off, how much review it saves, with confidence intervals.
gch probe            # does "fast" really mean no thinking?
gch pilot            # parse health + real token cost
gch run --split confirm
gch analyse --split confirm && gch confirm-report

Planned next: gch new-task --csv to turn a client's own labelled data into a task pack, and gch rerun for drift checks when a provider updates a model.

Uses
  • Mode advisor for LLM pipelines. Fast or thinking, per task, with the cost of each extra correct answer.
  • Sizing human review in ops contracts. The share of volume that can be auto-accepted at a stated precision.
  • Model-risk evidence for regulated clients. Pre-registered, out-of-sample, with intervals.
  • Drift monitoring. Re-run the same items when a provider ships a new version.
Limits
  • Stated confidence is the model's own number, not a true probability.
  • Three cheap-tier models and one prompt template. Results are about these tasks, not a model leaderboard.
  • Public datasets (Banking77, BLiMP, Civil Comments) may be in the models' training data.
  • Moderation labels are "at least half of annotators said toxic". Part of the moderation error is policy mismatch, not model failure.
  • Review and error costs are assumptions. Gemini prices come from third-party listings.
